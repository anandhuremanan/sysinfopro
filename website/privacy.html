Privacy policy

SysInfo Pro by Probably Fine Labs · Last updated 24 October 2026

SysInfo Pro is a system information and maintenance tool for Windows. It runs entirely on your PC. Probably Fine Labs does not collect, receive or store any information about you or your computer. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising and no telemetry.

What the app reads on your PC

To show its information, the app reads details about your computer: hardware, drives and free space, drive health (wear, temperature and running hours, as the drive reports them), running programs, network ports, startup programs, installed software, PATH entries, the disks of WSL and Docker, battery and security settings. This information is shown to you and is not sent anywhere.

What the app saves on your PC

  • Settings such as your theme, which readings the widget shows, your alert limits, the widget's keyboard shortcut, and the folder you last searched in Dev caches (used by the monthly cleanup reminder, if you turn it on).
  • Your previous PATH, saved when you remove entries from it, so the change can be undone. It is replaced the next time you change PATH and removed when you undo.
  • A cache for the app's window (Microsoft WebView2), which draws the app's screens.
  • Snapshots for "What changed": lists of startup programs, installed software, listening ports and PATH entries, kept so the app can show you what changed. They are stored in your user folder under AppData\Roaming\SysInfoPro, and the app keeps at most the last 20.
  • Files you choose to export, such as the spec card image and the system report, are saved to your Downloads folder. The system report includes your device's serial number; check it before sharing it.

All of this is kept in your user folder under AppData\Roaming\SysInfoPro. Uninstalling the app, or deleting that folder, removes it.

What the app changes on your PC

Only what you ask for, after you confirm: ending a program, deleting caches, temporary files and project folders you select, removing entries from your own PATH, removing Docker data Docker no longer uses, and updating apps through Windows Package Manager (winget).

Services the app contacts

Some features need the internet. When they run, your PC connects directly to the services below, which receive your IP address as part of any internet connection. Each service's own privacy policy applies.

  • Speed test (only when you start one): Cloudflare (speed.cloudflare.com and 1.1.1.1). If Cloudflare is unavailable, Measurement Lab (M-Lab) is used instead. M-Lab publishes all test results, including the IP address and the measured speeds, as open data for internet research.
  • Internet connection check: a brief connection to Google's public DNS server (8.8.8.8) to see whether you are online. No data is sent.
  • Network doctor (only when you open it or press Check): one ping to your own router, a name lookup of www.microsoft.com through your DNS servers, and Windows' own connectivity check (www.msftconnecttest.com), the same one Windows uses to show "No internet". If you type a site to test, the app looks up that site and opens one secure connection to it. It goes through your proxy if you have one, and nothing about you or your PC is sent.
  • Software version check (only when you open the Software tool): the app asks the publishers of common developer tools for their latest version numbers, from nodejs.org, go.dev, endoflife.date and GitHub. It does not send them your list of installed software.
  • App updates (only when you open the All apps list in the Software tool): Windows Package Manager (winget), part of Windows, checks Microsoft's winget catalogue for newer versions of your apps, and downloads an update from its publisher when you choose to install it.
  • Docker (only when you open WSL & Docker and Docker is installed): the app asks Docker on your PC how much space it uses. This stays on your PC.
  • Rating: if you choose to rate the app, the Microsoft Store opens. Nothing is sent before you choose.

Links you choose to open, such as a program's download page, open in your web browser.

Children

The app does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.
